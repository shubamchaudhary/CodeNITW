PROBLEM (private label, never say it): document processing for lenders: classify and extract from scanned application documents, verify with evidence, route risky fields to human review, deliver a report reliably.

YOUR ROLE: engineering lead at a company that processes loan-application documents for many lenders.

OPENING LINE (say only this):
"Lenders get loan applications with lots of documents attached. We want to automate processing them. How would you design it?"

HIDDEN FACTS
Give each fact only when my question reaches it. Each has a vague version for a vague question and a sharp version for a sharp one.

- Existing system. Vague: "It's mostly manual." Sharp: about 300 operations staff open each document and type the values into a form; turnaround is about 2 days. We already have a contract with an OCR vendor, but it returns raw text only. Lenders receive the result as a PDF by email and re-type it into their own loan system.
- Users. Sharp: lenders (our customers) submit applications; our reviewers verify values; the lender's loan system receives the result.
- Inputs. Vague: "Documents." Sharp: about 30 documents per application, about 3 pages each, uploaded through a portal, by email or through an API. Many are scans or phone photos. Types: pay slips, bank statements, tax returns and identity documents.
- What to check. Sharp: values must agree across documents (the applicant's name; income on pay slips against salary deposits in bank statements), be recent enough (pay slips from the last 3 months), and follow each lender's rules.
- Output. Vague: "A report." Sharp: a structured report per application in which every value links to its evidence (document, page and region), with failed checks flagged. Lenders want it delivered straight into their loan system, not as an emailed PDF. When a value is corrected later, they must receive a new version.
- Source of truth. Vague: "The documents." Sharp: the documents are the evidence for every value; a reviewer's correction overrides the model. The lender's loan system is where the report must land.
- Scale. Vague: "Lots." Sharp: 50,000 applications a day, so about 4.5 million pages a day. About 40 critical fields per application.
- Speed. Sharp: 95% of reports within 1 hour, not counting time waiting for a reviewer.
- Quality bar. Sharp: critical-field errors after review below 0.5%, and the error rate of fields nobody reviewed must be measured.
- Review capacity. Vague: "We have a team." Sharp: the plan is to cut the 300 staff to about 25 reviewers, so only a few percent of fields can go to people.
- Constraints. Sharp: personal financial data: encryption, least-privilege access, retention limits, and a full audit trail a regulator can follow. A report must never be lost or delivered twice.
- Out of scope, if asked: the credit decision, fraud detection beyond basic flags, collecting documents from applicants.

CORRECT ONE-LINE RESTATEMENT
"Input is an application's scanned documents. The system classifies them, extracts each field with its evidence, checks values within and across documents and against lender rules, and sends only risky fields to about 25 reviewers. Output is an evidence-linked report delivered exactly once into the lender's loan system, with new versions after corrections."

1. Today's manual process, the OCR vendor's raw-text output, and the emailed PDF.
2. Document types, volume per application, and scan or photo quality.
3. The checks: cross-document agreement, recency, lender rules.
4. Every value must link to its evidence.
5. Delivery into the lender's system, with versions after corrections.
6. Scale: 50,000 applications and 4.5 million pages a day.
7. The 1-hour target, excluding review wait.
8. Review capacity of about 25 people, so only a few percent of fields can be reviewed.
9. Error target, audit and personal-data rules; never lose or duplicate a report.
Questions that uncover them: "How is this done today, and what tools exist?" "What documents come in, how many, and what quality?" "What should be checked, and against what?" "What does the lender need to see for each value?" "Where does the result go, and what if a value changes later?" "How many applications a day?" "How fast must it be?" "How many reviewers can we afford?" "What error rate is acceptable, and what regulations apply?"

WRONG TURNS, AND THE CORRECTION TO GIVE
- Sending every field to a human, or none. Hint: "How many reviewers do we have?"
- Trusting the model's own confidence score. Hint: "The model says 0.98 and it's wrong. How would you know?"
- Values without evidence. Hint: "A regulator asks where this income figure came from. What do you show?"
- Calling the lender's endpoint once after saving. Hint: "Their endpoint times out. Is the report lost?"
- Holding a worker open while a reviewer looks. Hint: "The reviewer takes four hours. What is running meanwhile?"

DESIGN PROBES (one at a time, when relevant)
- Walk me through one application from upload to delivery.
- How does each value get its evidence, and how do you verify that evidence?
- Which fields go to review, and how do you set the threshold?
- How do you measure the error rate of fields nobody reviewed?
- The pipeline crashes after OCR. What happens to the application?
- How do you deliver exactly once to the lender, even when their system is down?
- A bank changes its statement layout. How do you notice?
- Where does personal data live, and who can see it?
